## Design notes for the group (delete before distributing)

### How the merge was done

All three sources went into one pool, then every overlapping construct was resolved down to a single question so nobody answers the same thing twice. The main clusters that got merged:

- **Budgeting discipline scale.** Kept the 10-item battery in full (Section H) rather than mixing it with a second, shorter self-report scale — a scale should stay intact rather than get cherry-picked, or its internal consistency breaks. My original 5-item version is folded in: 3 items were near-duplicates and dropped, 1 ("wait before buying") moved into the online section as the concrete cart-wait question, 1 ("know how much I spent last week") dropped in favor of the sharper ৳500-accuracy item.
- **Online impulse-buying scale.** Merged both Likert sets into one 6-item battery. Discount/flash-sale/countdown-timer became one item instead of three, since they're the same urgency mechanism. The "compare prices before buying online" reverse-item was dropped — it's the same construct as "I compare prices before making a purchase" already in the budgeting scale.
- **Running out of money.** This was asked three different ways across the three sources (a frequency scale, a reverse-scored Likert item, a direct question). Kept only the reverse-scored Likert item (Section H) plus the follow-up "what do you do about it" (behavioural, not redundant).
- **Family occupation / income / education.** These are three separate SES proxies, not duplicates — kept all three, they let you cross-check when one is skipped or "don't know."
- **Windfall ৳5,000 question.** Appeared in two of the three sources. Kept the version with more response options (7 vs. 4) since it captures more variation.
- **Peer/ad influence on impulse buying.** Was three separate items across sources (do ads influence you / do peers influence you / does seeing a classmate's lifestyle make you question spending). Replaced with one forced-choice comparative item ("which is more likely...") plus the general social-media-influence Likert item. Forced choice gives you a direct ranking instead of two Likert means that are hard to compare against each other.
- **bKash-balance-awareness item.** Dropped — it assumes everyone uses mobile financial services, and it measures the same underlying thing as the ৳500-accuracy item.

### New items worth knowing about

A few additions from the new list are genuinely strong and change what the survey can say:

- **Support flow reversed** (Section E, Q31–32): most surveys assume money flows family → student. Asking whether the student sends money *to* family is a real and common pattern here, and it's a variable your teammates hadn't captured.
- **Birth order + siblings** (Section B): lets you test whether birth order predicts financial responsibility independent of income.
- **Session jam / exam delay** (Section D, Q28): ties financial disruption directly to a structural feature of Bangladeshi higher ed that varies systematically by your university-type variable — this could be one of your more interesting cross-tabs.
- **Hiding purchases from family** (Section C, Q23): a transparency/social-desirability measure that should correlate with living arrangement in a testable way (students at home vs. away).
- **৳500 spending-accuracy item** (Section H, Q55): a behavioural proxy for financial awareness rather than a self-rated trait, less prone to people just picking "agree" to look responsible.

### Length — read this before you finalize

This merged draft is **78 items**, up from 42–48 in the earlier drafts. Even with mostly single-tap questions, that's realistically 13–16 minutes, not the 8–10 you were aiming for. Length is now the single biggest hesitation risk in this survey — more than any individual question's phrasing.

I've tagged the **lowest-priority items** below with 🔹 — these are interesting but not load-bearing for your core question (living arrangement × background → financial habits/budgeting/impulse buying). If your pilot shows people dropping off or rushing the back half, cut from this tag first. Cutting all 🔹 items brings you to roughly 62 items.

I'd genuinely recommend piloting the full 78-item version once, timing it, and deciding the cut list from real data rather than guessing now — but the tags are there if you want to pre-decide.

### Scale honesty (carried over, still applies)

The budgeting-discipline and impulse-buying Likert batteries are **not verified against the original published instruments** — they're written in the spirit of the self-control and impulse-buying literature (e.g. Rook & Fisher). Report them as researcher-developed / adapted items with your own pilot Cronbach's alpha, not as validated scales, unless someone on the team checks the wording against the source papers and cites it properly.

### Sampling (carried over, still applies)

Convenience/snowball sample, distributed across the five of you by university-type segment. Report the realised sample's skew by university type, city, and gender — don't claim national representativeness.
